PROPOSED RESEARCH QUESTION

Among business decision-makers responding at the event, what oversight practices and information sources accompany their reported use of generative AI?

Exploratory self-report pilot. The form does not measure actual security, legal compliance, or technical quantum readiness.

01

What changed

The main form keeps six short AI questions. Business size, barriers, and free text remain removed. Two optional quantum questions are restored in a separate section on the same page.

The header shows six AI questions plus two optional questions and an estimated three-minute length. Check timing in pretesting. No respondent names or contact fields are included.

Only respondents who report trial or regular AI use see all six main questions. Nonusers can still answer the two optional quantum questions. The review notes stay on this separate link.

03

Branching and analysis

  1. Q1 asks whether the respondent owns a business or helps choose its technology. “No” ends politely. A skipped role does not establish eligibility.
  2. Q2 asks about the past 30 days. Trial and regular use automatically reveal Q3–Q6 on the same page. Nonuse, uncertainty, or a missing answer do not enter the current-user analysis.
  3. Q3 and Q6 allow multiple choices. “Not sure” and the relevant “No one” or “We haven’t checked” option cannot be combined with other answers.
  4. Report respondents and item-specific counts. Keep missing, skipped, uncertain, and negative answers distinct. Request one respondent per business in the finalized invitation, but do not claim verified unique businesses.
  5. Use descriptive summaries for this self-selected event sample. Do not generalize to all local businesses, create a validated risk score, or infer that the event caused a change.
04

Before response collection

Private response storage is implemented. The owner controls whether collection is open from the separate dashboard. With collection closed, the form is a preview and does not save selections. When open, submitted answers are stored in the private database and can be exported by the owner as CSV. Paper responses can be entered through the protected dashboard.

  1. Determine whether institutional review applies, and complete any required review before collection.
  2. Finalize brief participant information: purpose, adults 18+, voluntary participation, investigator contact, privacy limits, data access and retention, and any required consent. Disclose Aulden’s commercial role and keep sales follow-up separate.
  3. Set up an approved collection method and check the final wording and timing. Where feasible, collect before the substantive presentation; otherwise report actual timing.

The response database stores a random receipt, submission time, instrument and participant-information versions, source (web or paper), a role-eligibility flag, and selected answers. It does not store respondent names, emails, company names, IP addresses, or user-agent strings. Platform request metadata may still exist separately. Never promise absolute anonymity. Set and follow the appropriate retention plan before opening collection.
